# Inclusive Design Reflection

## Research & Learn

### Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?
Vulnerable populations can include neurodivergent people such as those with ADHD or Autism, people 
with mental health conditions, older adults, people with disabilities, and anyone who struggles 
with executive functioning. In digital spaces they may face challenges like overwhelming 
interfaces, unclear instructions, too much information at once, or apps that assume everyone 
processes information the same way.

### What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals?
Avoiding overwhelming or cluttered interfaces, respecting sensory needs like avoiding harsh 
colours or sounds, using clear and predictable navigation, and communicating in a direct and 
simple way rather than relying on implied meaning.

### How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism?
Using simple, direct language, keeping navigation predictable and consistent, breaking tasks 
into smaller steps, reducing unnecessary visual clutter, and avoiding sudden changes or surprises 
in the interface.

### How can we support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting?
By communicating clearly and directly, giving advance notice before meetings or big questions, 
respecting different working styles and paces, and allowing flexibility instead of expecting 
everyone to work the exact same way.

## Reflection

### How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?
I can be more direct and clear in my messages, avoid vague or ambiguous language, and give people 
time to process questions instead of expecting instant answers, since I personally know how 
stressful sudden questions can feel.

### What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?
Things like having too many settings and options visible at once, features that feel repetitive 
or static (like the Pep Talk quotes I noticed earlier), or notifications and prompts that feel 
intrusive rather than supportive, such as the AI blocker interrupting even in Chill Mode.

### What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?
I can pay closer attention to how much cognitive load a feature adds when I'm working on the app, 
and think about whether instructions or flows are simple and predictable enough for someone who 
might get overwhelmed easily.

## Task

### First-person account read
I read a first-person account from Jo, 35, who was diagnosed with ADHD at 28 after struggling for 
years at work with new procedures, and even had to push her GP for a referral since he initially 
denied ADHD existed. She described the diagnosis as a huge relief, since she had spent her whole 
life being labelled as lazy and had started to believe it herself. She also felt sad that she 
wasn't diagnosed earlier, wondering how different her life could have been. After diagnosis, she 
became kinder to herself, joined a support group, and eventually tried medication, which helps 
her set goals, focus, and stay on track, though she only uses it when she really needs to focus 
because of the side effects.

### One design or communication improvement that could make Focus Bear more accessible
Jo's story shows how someone can go undiagnosed for years while quietly struggling and blaming 
themselves. This makes me think Focus Bear should be extra careful with its tone, especially 
around missed tasks or broken streaks, since users may already carry a lot of self doubt. Instead 
of neutral or blunt messaging, the app could use more encouraging, judgement free language when a 
user misses a task or falls behind, similar to how Jo described finally being treated with 
patience and support once she understood her ADHD.

### Practice response to a hypothetical user struggling with executive functioning
"Hey, it's completely okay if you're struggling with this. You don't have to tackle everything at 
once. Let's just pick one small task, even something tiny, and focus on that first. It's okay if 
today feels harder than other days, that doesn't mean you're doing anything wrong. You got this, 
don't stress."
